## 1. Standard process — ordered steps and named deliverables

The de-facto standard is MIT's Disciplined Entrepreneurship (Aulet) for the customer→TAM chain [S18][S19], Moore for beachhead logic [S21], Dunford for competitive alternatives [S8], Porter/Kim-Mauborgne for industry structure and value curves [S1][S2][S4][S5], and Sequoia/YC for what investors will actually check [S7][S11][S13].

| # | Step | What a competent practitioner does | Named deliverable |
|---|------|-------------------------------------|-------------------|
| 0 | Fix the counting unit and frame | Decide *what is being counted* (end user, buying unit, establishment, enterprise, household), the geography, and the statistical frame (e.g., PSA List of Establishments vs ASPBI formal sector — they differ 4.4×, see §4). PSA's own definition: "Establishment is defined as an economic unit under a single ownership or control which engages in one or predominantly one kind of economic activity at a single fixed location" [S29]. | **Sizing Charter** (unit, frame, PSIC codes, geography, currency, reference year) |
| 1 | Market segmentation (DE Step 1) | Brainstorm end-user groups broadly, narrow to 6–12 candidate segments, do primary interviews per segment [S18][S20]. | **Segmentation Matrix** |
| 2 | Select a beachhead (DE Step 2) | Score candidates on Aulet's seven criteria: customer budget/willingness to pay, accessibility, compelling reason to buy, ability to deliver a whole product now, competition, expansion potential, founder fit [S20 — secondary summary of S18]. Moore's test: "a target segment that is big enough to matter, small enough to lead, and a good fit with your crown jewels" [S21]. | **Beachhead Selection Memo** (scores + evidence refs) |
| 3 | End User Profile (DE Step 3) | Demographics, behaviours, motivations, current way of solving the problem, "as vivid and specific as possible" [S20]. | **End User Profile** |
| 4 | Beachhead TAM (DE Step 4) | Bottom-up: count end users from "customer lists, industry databases, trade associations, or public records"; top-down cross-check against "industry reports, census data, or analyst estimates"; TAM = number of end users × annual revenue per user [S20]. Investors: "bottom-up should be your primary approach when presenting to investors", top-down is "a rough directional check" [S17]. | **Market Size Ledger** + **Triangulation Sheet** |
| 5 | Persona (DE Step 5) | One real person, ranked purchasing priorities [S20]. (Feeds positioning/pricing domains.) | **Persona Sheet** |
| 6 | Competitive alternatives inventory | Ask "What would a customer do if your offering didn't exist?"; "the first competitive alternative we need to beat is the status quo" — spreadsheets, manual processes, incumbent platform's built-in features [S8]. Sequoia: "Who are your direct and indirect competitors" [S7]. | **Alternatives Register** (incl. do-nothing / DIY rows) |
| 7 | Competitor feature/price matrix + dated price snapshots | Same feature list across all alternatives; every price captured with plan, metric, billing period, date, archived snapshot (Wayback `web.archive.org/web/YYYYMMDDhhmmss/URL` [S24]). | **Competitor Matrix** + **Price Snapshot Ledger** |
| 8 | Industry structure & attractiveness | Five Forces: rivals, customers, suppliers, potential entrants, substitutes — "The Five Forces determine the competitive structure of an industry, and its profitability" [S2]; nine-box factors (industry attractiveness × competitive strength) [S15]; Kevin Hale's problem tests: popular, growing, urgent, expensive, mandatory, frequent [S11]. | **Industry Structure Brief** |
| 9 | Timing ("why now") | Sequoia: "Nature hates a vacuum—so why hasn't your solution been built before now?" [S7]; Gross: "Timing accounted for 42 percent of the difference between success and failure" [S14]. List dated enabling changes. | **Why-Now Sheet** |
| 10 | Positioning artifacts | Strategy canvas / value curve (Kim & Mauborgne) + Four Actions (eliminate/reduce/raise/create) [S4][S5]; positioning map (2-axis perceptual map, ideally from customer perception data, not manager judgment) [S22]; DE Step 11 "Chart Your Competitive Position" [S18]. | **Strategy Canvas** + **Positioning Map** |
| 11 | Follow-on markets (DE Step 14) | Adjacent "bowling pins" sharing customer traits, channel or whole-product components [S18][S21]. | **Follow-on Market List** |
| 12 | Source registry & refresh | Every number: source org, title, URL, publication date, reference period, cadence, retrieved date, archived URL, grade. Refresh on the source's cadence. | **Source Registry** |

## 2. Frameworks & methods

| Framework | Originator | Produces | Use when | Known critiques |
|-----------|-----------|----------|----------|-----------------|
| TAM / SAM / SOM | Investor/consulting convention (no single originator found) | Nested market layers: TAM "maximum potential demand of a specific market"; SAM "the size of the TAM you can reasonably target"; SOM "the size of the SAM you can potentially convert" [S16] | Any external claim of opportunity size; investor decks | Layers are only as good as the factors; SOM often reverse-engineered from a wished revenue [S9] |
| Top-down sizing | Consulting convention | Macro figure × filters (segment %, geography %, channel %) [S16][S17]; Statista describes it as global size "allocated to countries using drivers (e.g. GDP, digitization etc)" [S25] | Sanity check; when no list of units exists | "relies heavily on assumptions" [S16]; single-source TAMs; the "1% of a huge market" trap [S9] |
| Bottom-up sizing | Convention; codified by Aulet (DE Step 4) | Units counted from named lists × annual revenue per unit [S20]; "Market Size = ACV × Number of Potential Customers" [S17] | Primary method; beachhead TAM | Time-intensive [S16]; unit list must be real, dated and sourced |
| Value-theory sizing | Practitioner consensus; originator **UNVERIFIED** (named in secondary guides only) | Size = value delivered to customer × share of value capturable (willingness to pay) | Category-creating products where no comparable spend exists (Sequoia: "Some of the best companies invent their own markets" [S7]) | Depends on quantified value proposition (DE Step 8); easy to inflate |
| Triangulation rule | Practitioner consensus (Aulet Step 4 "top-down validation" [S20]; Forum VC [S17]; Statista's own "Reconciliation & Alignment" of top-down and bottom-up [S25]) | Two or more independent methods landing in the same range | Always, before any number leaves the team | No canonical tolerance; harness must set one (see §5) |
| Disciplined Entrepreneurship 24 Steps | Bill Aulet, Martin Trust Center, MIT Sloan [S19]; step titles verified from book TOC [S18] | Steps 1–5: Market Segmentation → Select a Beachhead Market → Build an End User Profile → Calculate the TAM Size for the Beachhead Market → Profile the Persona; Step 11 Chart Your Competitive Position; Step 14 TAM for Follow-on Markets; Steps 20–21 Identify/Test Key Assumptions [S18] | Whole idea→customer→TAM chain; the harness's spine | Book's "$20–100M/yr" beachhead-TAM range comes from a secondary summary [S20] — **UNVERIFIED against the primary**; US-centric scale, needs PH recalibration |
| Crossing the Chasm / bowling alley | Geoffrey Moore | Beachhead "big enough to matter, small enough to lead, good fit with your crown jewels"; pragmatists need "a compelling reason to buy"; four GTM playbooks (Early Market, Bowling Alley, Tornado, Main Street) [S21] | B2B tech with pragmatist buyers | Threshold for "chasm crossed" not stated in source read |
| Porter's Five Forces | Michael E. Porter (HBR 1979/2008) [S1] | Industry structure and profitability; "assess industry attractiveness, how trends will affect industry competition, which industries a company should compete in" [S2] | Attractiveness of the beachhead industry; supplier/buyer power for a hardware+software studio | Critics (Coyne & Subramaniam): assumes actors "do not interact and collude", value comes from structural barriers, "uncertainty is low"; ignores complementors (Brandenburger & Nalebuff / Grove); gives no direct management prescription [S3]. Porter himself: managers "define competition too narrowly" [S1]; "Industry structure changes over time, and is not static" [S2]. Porter's warning that growth rate ≠ attractiveness is in the 2008 article but **UNVERIFIED** here (full text paywalled) |
| GE–McKinsey nine-box | McKinsey for GE, early 1970s [S15] | 3×3 map of industry attractiveness × competitive strength; above-diagonal invest, diagonal selective, below-diagonal harvest/sell [S15] | Choosing among several candidate segments/product lines | "judgment is required to weigh the trade-offs" [S15]; factor weights are subjective |
| Competitive alternatives | April Dunford | List of what the buyer would do instead, starting with status quo; "In enterprise software, we typically lose between 20% and 30% of deals to 'no decision'" [S8] | Every B2B positioning; input to matrix in step 7 | None substantive; commonly skipped in favour of "direct competitors only" |
| Competitor feature/price matrix | Practitioner consensus | Same feature rows across all alternatives + price per metric + dated snapshot | Pricing, positioning, win/loss | Undated captures; feature names not normalised; missing do-nothing column |
| Positioning / perceptual map | Marketing research (multidimensional scaling; Ries & Trout popularised "positioning") [S22] | 2-axis map of perceived positions | Communicating differentiation | Only two dimensions; manager-made "judgmental maps" are "limited by not being based on consumer data" [S22] |
| Blue Ocean strategy canvas + Four Actions | W. Chan Kim & Renée Mauborgne [S4][S5] | Value curve across "the range of factors that an industry competes on and invests in" [S4]; Four Actions: "Which factors that the industry takes for granted should be eliminated? …reduced well below…raised well above…never offered should be created?" [S5] | Designing an offer that avoids head-on feature parity | "descriptive rather than prescriptive"; examples chosen post hoc, "unfalsifiable"; rebrands existing differentiation concepts [S6] |
| Startup-idea tests | Kevin Hale, YC | Problem must be popular, growing, urgent, expensive, mandatory, frequent; ideal: "millions of users", "markets that are growing 20% a year", "billions of dollars" [S11]; "X for Y" framing where "Y should be a huge market" [S12] | Screening candidate segments fast | Aspirational thresholds; YC's own deck guide asks only "Is it going to be big? Will you make it big?" [S13] |
| Timing analysis | Bill Gross (Idealab), TED 2015 | Five factors scored on "all 100 Idealab companies, and 100 non-Idealab companies"; timing 42%, then team/execution, idea, business model, funding [S14] | Why-now sheet | Small retrospective sample, single scorer |
| Big Market Delusion | Cornell & Damodaran, Financial Analysts Journal 2020 [S10] | Explains why every entrant into a big market gets overvalued: "each business cluster … will overestimate its capacity and its probability of success"; participants "downplay existing competition" | Guard-rail when a TAM looks huge | Finance-side framing; still the best-sourced statement of the 1% fallacy |

### 4.2 Verified PH market-structure facts

- **Two "how many businesses" answers, 4.4× apart.** LE-based DTI count: 1,241,476 establishments (2024) [S27]; ASPBI formal-sector count: 281,825 (2022) [S29]. Formal-sector scope excludes single proprietorships with no branches and TE<10 [S29]. A B2B beachhead count must declare which frame it uses.
- **Micro dominates counts, large dominates money.** Micro = 90.66% of establishments but 34.25% of employment (DTI 2024) [S27]; in the formal sector large establishments (1.5% of units) earn 50.2% of revenue and pay 61.9% of compensation [S29].
- **Sector shape (LE 2023):** wholesale/retail 604,758 units; accommodation & food 190,965; manufacturing 141,266; ICT (section J) only 11,247 units, of which 144 large; admin & support (N, incl. BPO) 18,661 units with 1.33M employed, 85% in large firms [S28]. Hardware buyers in manufacturing: 1,086 large + 978 medium establishments nationally [S28].
- **Geography:** NCR 18.00%, CALABARZON 15.41%, Central Luzon 12.67% of establishments (2024) [S27].
- **Same-name metrics differ by definition:** "electronics exports 2024" = $42.75B (SEIPI series) [S45] vs $39.1B (PSA "electronic products") [S46]; record the definition with every number.
- **Formal e-commerce is small in the formal sector:** ₱159.26B sales (2022), 81.7% by large establishments [S29].
- **Digital reach vs financial reach:** 83.8% internet penetration [S34] and 89% adult internet use [S41], but only 50% of adults own a transaction account; e-money (36%) exceeds bank accounts (23%) [S41].
- **Freshness:** ASPBI lags ~22 months; CPBI lands every ~5–6 years; LE/DTI annual; trade monthly; DataReportal annual; Statista semi-annual model refresh.

## 5. Mechanical gate candidates

Ledgers are CSV; a script reads them and emits PASS/FAIL per rule; human rows are explicit `signoff` records with name + date + hash of the ledger version signed.

**G1 — Sizing Charter gate** · Artifact `sizing_charter.csv`: `charter_id, counting_unit, unit_definition_source_url, frame (LE|ASPBI|CPBI|other), psic_codes, geography, currency, reference_year, created_at`. Check: all fields non-empty; `frame` in allowed set; `psic_codes` match `^[A-U](\d{2,4})?$`. Human: choice of unit and frame (sign-off row).

**G2 — Segment scoring gate** · `segments.csv`: `segment_id, end_user, description, score_budget, score_access, score_compelling_reason, score_whole_product, score_competition, score_expansion, score_founder_fit, evidence_refs, interview_count, status (candidate|beachhead|follow_on)`. Check: ≥6 rows with `status≠rejected`; exactly one `beachhead`; every score 1–5 with ≥1 `evidence_refs` pointing to interview IDs in the customer-discovery ledger; beachhead `interview_count ≥ 10` (harness default; Aulet only says "primary research" [S20]). Human: the beachhead decision and founder-fit score.

**G3 — Market-size factor gate** · `market_size_factors.csv`: `claim_id, layer (TAM|SAM|SOM), segment_id, method (top_down|bottom_up|value_theory), factor (unit_count|price_per_unit|frequency|adoption_share|filter), value, unit, currency, source_id, source_reference_period, source_publication_date, retrieved_at, archived_url, grade (P1|P2|P3), note` and `market_size_claims.csv`: `claim_id, stated_value, currency, computed_value, method, layer`. Checks: (a) `computed_value` = product of the claim's factor rows within 1% of `stated_value`; (b) every factor row has `source_id`, `retrieved_at`, `archived_url` (regex `^https://web\.archive\.org/web/\d{14}/`) and `grade`; (c) `bottom_up` unit_count factor is P1/P2 (named list/registry), never P3; (d) SOM ≤ SAM ≤ TAM; (e) no `adoption_share` factor in a SOM claim lacks a bottom-up anchor (flags "1% of market" [S9]); (f) staleness: `source_reference_period` older than 36 months for census/LE data or 24 months for annual series → WARN, 60 months → FAIL. Human: adoption assumptions and price-per-unit (until the pricing domain's ledger supplies them).

**G4 — Triangulation gate** · derived from G3: for each `(layer, segment_id)` require ≥2 distinct `method`s; PASS if `max/min ≤ 3.0` (harness default; no canonical value exists — practitioner consensus) and every method's factors pass G3. Human: explanation row when the gap exceeds tolerance and the team proceeds anyway.

**G5 — Competitive alternatives & price-snapshot gate** · `alternatives.csv`: `alt_id, name, type (direct|indirect|status_quo|diy|do_nothing), url, why_customer_uses_it, evidence_refs` and `price_snapshots.csv`: `snapshot_id, alt_id, plan_name, list_price, currency, billing_period, price_metric (per_seat|per_device|per_site|flat|usage), included_limits, discount_terms, source_url, archived_url, captured_at, captured_by, screenshot_hash`. Checks: ≥1 row each of `status_quo|diy|do_nothing` [S8]; ≥3 non-status-quo alternatives; every alternative with `type≠do_nothing` has ≥1 snapshot ≤90 days old; each snapshot has `archived_url` regex + `screenshot_hash`; currency/period normalised to ₱ per month in a derived column. Human: "compelling reason to buy" statement per alternative; any price obtained by quote/email (record `captured_by`).

**G6 — Feature-matrix completeness** · `feature_matrix.csv`: `feature_id, feature_name, <alt_id columns>` values in `{yes,no,partial,unknown}`. Check: same feature list for all alternatives; `unknown` ≤10% of cells; every `yes` for a competitor has an evidence URL in `feature_evidence.csv`. Human: which features are table-stakes vs differentiators (feeds strategy canvas).

**G7 — Industry structure & attractiveness gate** · `five_forces.csv`: `force, rating_1to5, evidence_refs, source_ids` (five rows exactly) and `attractiveness.csv`: `factor (market_size|growth_rate|profitability|entry_barriers|regulation|buyer_concentration), value, unit, source_id, reference_period`. Checks: all five forces present with ≥1 P1/P2 source each; `growth_rate` present with dated source; no factor row without `source_id`. Human: overall attractiveness verdict (nine-box placement) [S15].

**G8 — Why-now gate** · `why_now.csv`: `signal_id, what_changed, metric, value_before, value_after, date_before, date_after, source_id`. Check: ≥2 signals with both dates and sources. Human: judgment that the window is open.

**G9 — Source registry gate** · `sources.csv`: `source_id, org, title, url, publication_date, reference_period, cadence, retrieved_at, archived_url, fetch_status (live|archived|manual), content_hash, grade`. Checks: every `source_id` referenced anywhere exists here; `grade` ∈ {P1,P2,P3}; `archived_url` or `content_hash` present (PSA/IMF pages are CAPTCHA-gated [operational finding]); a P3-only claim in any TAM factor → FAIL; `retrieved_at` older than the source's `cadence` → WARN "refresh due". Human: none — fully mechanical.

**Cannot be mechanised (must remain sign-off rows):** beachhead choice; definition of the counting unit; adoption/penetration assumptions; "compelling reason to buy"; attractiveness verdict; timing judgment; any decision to proceed despite a failed triangulation.

## 6. Early irreversibles

1. **Counting unit and statistical frame (G1).** Every ledger (TAM factors, competitor counts, persona, pricing metric) inherits it; changing from "establishment (LE)" to "formal-sector establishment (ASPBI)" moves the base by 4.4× [S27][S29] and invalidates all downstream numbers.
2. **Beachhead segment (DE Step 2).** Product spec (Step 7), value proposition (Step 8), next-10 customers (Step 9), DMU (Step 12), sales process (Steps 13/18) all derive from it [S18]; Moore: the team must run one playbook at a time — "what works in one stage will likely underperform in others" [S21].
3. **Competitive alternative / category framing.** Dunford: alternatives are "the starting point for understanding what differentiates your product" [S8]; positioning, canvas axes and price anchors all follow.
4. **Price metric (per seat/device/site/flat).** Fixes the "annual revenue per user" factor in TAM [S20] and the comparison basis for every competitor snapshot (G5).
5. **Geography claimed.** Including markets the studio cannot sell into inflates TAM and is the classic pitch "lie" family [S9] — practitioner consensus calls this the "China syndrome" (**UNVERIFIED attribution**).
6. **Whether the harness treats PH as the total market or as beachhead + export follow-on.** Determines which sources are load-bearing (PSA/DTI vs global analyst models) and therefore the refresh cadence and grade rules in G9.

## 7. Failure modes / anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Guard in harness | Source |
|--------------|------------------|--------|
| "All we have to do is get 1% of the market" / "(Big name research firm) says our market will be $50 billion" | G3(e), G4; SOM must be built bottom-up | Kawasaki 2006 [S9] |
| Big Market Delusion — every entrant assumes it wins; competition "downplay[ed]" | G5 requires ≥3 alternatives + status quo; G7 rivalry force sourced | Cornell & Damodaran 2020 [S10] |
| Top-down-only TAM from one analyst chart | G4 ≥2 methods; G3(c) bottom-up count must be P1/P2 | Forum VC [S17]; "single Gartner chart" quote attributed to a YC partner — **UNVERIFIED**, practitioner consensus |
| Ignoring do-nothing/DIY; 20–30% of enterprise deals lost to "no decision" | G5 status-quo rows mandatory | Dunford [S8] |
| Static Five Forces snapshot; forces listed without evidence | G7 evidence per force; re-run on cadence; "Industry structure changes over time, and is not static" | ISC-HBS [S2]; critiques [S3] |
| Treating growth rate as attractiveness | G7 requires profitability/entry-barrier factors, not growth alone | Porter 2008 (wording **UNVERIFIED**, paywalled); practitioner consensus |
| Blue-ocean storytelling after the fact | Canvas must be built from G6 feature matrix, not adjectives | Wikipedia critique [S6] |
| Citing Statista/Euromonitor modeled numbers as observed PH data | G9 grade rule: aggregator = P3 unless original source traced; Statista itself: small economies modeled from "benchmark ratios from similar, more developed markets" | Statista [S25]; USD libguide [S26] |
| Mixing definitions of the same metric (electronics exports $42.75B vs $39.1B) | G3 `unit`/`note` fields + source-definition text required | [S45][S46] |
| Mixing frames (LE 1.24M vs ASPBI 281,825) | G1 frame field; G3 rejects factors from a different frame than the charter | [S27][S29] |
| Stale data (ASPBI ~22-month lag; CPBI ~5–6-year cycle) | G3(f), G9 refresh warnings | [S29][S31] |
| Double-counted users (social identities ≠ people; mobile connections 122% of population) | G3 unit must be "persons/establishments", never "identities/connections" for unit_count | DataReportal caveats [S34] |
| Judgmental positioning maps with no customer data | G6/G2 evidence refs; map axes must trace to interview codes | Wikipedia [S22] |
| Undated competitor prices / prices without plan metric | G5 archived snapshot + `price_metric` mandatory | Wayback format [S24]; practitioner consensus |
| Reverse-engineered share ("we need ₱X so share = X/TAM") | G3(e) | Kawasaki [S9]; practitioner consensus |
| Scripted fetch silently reading a CAPTCHA page as "the source" | G9 `content_hash` + `fetch_status`; reject pages < N bytes or containing "verification" | operational finding, this brief |
